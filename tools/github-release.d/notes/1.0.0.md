TaffyGo is a Chromium browser for Android with ad and tracker blocking built in and one AI assistant, Taffy. You bring the AI: a key of your own, or a plan you already pay for. There is no account to create and no TaffyGo server behind the app.

This is TaffyGo 1.0, built on Chromium 152.0.7977.42.

## What it does

- Blocks ads and trackers in every tab from the first page you open, using the EasyList and EasyPrivacy rules. You can turn blocking off for a site that breaks.
- Answers questions about the page you are reading, or about several tabs at once.
- Takes on a piece of research in its own tabs. You can watch it work and take over at any time, and the task stops where it is.
- Collects results in a workspace, with the source beside every fact. Keep what you want in your Library.
- Asks before it types into a form or saves a file. When a site wants you to sign in, Taffy hands you the page, and it asks you for any code only you have.
- Never sends passwords, one-time codes, payment details or passkeys to an AI provider.
- Works as a complete browser without the assistant: tabs, private tabs, bookmarks, history, downloads, and one address bar for sites, searches and questions.

## Setting up Taffy

Taffy is not set up on a fresh install, and the browser works without it. To use it, open Settings, then AI and providers. Add a key from one of 38 providers, or sign in to a plan you already pay for with a provider that offers one. Your keys stay on your phone. Requests go from your phone straight to the provider you chose, and any cost is between you and that provider.

## What leaves your phone

- Pages you open are requested from the sites that serve them, as in any browser.
- When you ask Taffy something, your question and the text of the pages it reads go to your AI provider. A provider on the internet is reached only over HTTPS. A server on your own local network may use plain HTTP if you set it up that way.
- Some of Chromium's own services stay on. Searches go to Google, the default search engine, and you can choose another in Settings. Safe Browsing checks run through Google Play services on the phone. Chromium's component updater fetches security data, such as certificate revocation lists, from Google.
- Nothing goes to TaffyGo, because TaffyGo has no server to receive it.

## Requirements

- Android 10 or later.
- A phone with a 64-bit ARM processor (arm64-v8a). There is no build for 32-bit ARM or x86.

## Installing

1. Download `TaffyGo-1.0.0-arm64.apk` from the assets below and check it against `SHA256SUMS` and the signing certificate (see Verify).
2. Open the file on your phone. Android asks you to allow installs from the app you downloaded it with. You can turn that permission off again once TaffyGo is installed.
3. This copy does not update itself. New versions are published on this releases page.

If you install TaffyGo from Google Play instead, that copy is signed by Google's app signing key, so the Play copy and this one cannot update each other. To switch, uninstall the one you have first. Uninstalling deletes everything TaffyGo keeps on the phone, including your history, workspaces and saved keys. Settings, then Privacy, then Delete everything does the same without uninstalling.

## Known limitations

- TaffyGo is laid out and tested for phones held upright. Turned sideways, the start page's field is cut off and the page menu does not scroll. It installs on tablets, but Taffy's screens are not designed for a larger screen.
- There is one browser profile. Backup can make a backup and check it, but cannot restore one yet.
- Taffy's own screens are in English and Hindi. The rest of the browser uses Chromium's translations.
- There is no sync. Everything stays on the one phone.
- Websites cannot send push notifications.
- The blocking rules are the EasyList and EasyPrivacy lists of 27 August 2026, shipped inside the app. They change only when the app is updated.
- On a phone without Google Play services, Safe Browsing protection is limited.
- Taffy can be wrong, and pages can fail to load. Taffy reports what it found and what it could not, and says when a task is only partly done.
